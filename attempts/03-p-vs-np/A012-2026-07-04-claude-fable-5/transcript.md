### proof-complexity-sos (sub-agent, verbatim)

The Lasserre/SOS world genuinely delivers what C-002 says cannot
exist — up to a crucial reinterpretation of "faithful." The
feasible regions of the Lasserre hierarchy are spectrahedra:
positive-dimensional, real semialgebraic, convex objects, whose
geometry carries robust complexity invariants. Extension
complexity (nonnegative rank of the slack matrix, Yannakakis),
psd rank, and SOS degree are all continuous-geometric quantities,
and they provably track *some* hardness: Grigoriev showed degree
Omega(n) Positivstellensatz lower bounds for knapsack and for
F2-linear (parity/Tseitin) systems; Schoenebeck showed random
3-XOR and 3-SAT need Omega(n) Lasserre rounds; Fiorini et al. and
Rothvoss showed exponential extension complexity for the TSP and
matching polytopes; Lee-Raghavendra-Steurer showed exponential
psd rank for the correlation polytope. These are unconditional,
positive-dimensional, and they do not relativize in the trivial
sense nor trip Razborov-Rudich (they are lower bounds against a
concrete restricted model, targeting specific instance families,
so "largeness" never enters). In the narrow sense, the trigger
condition — positive-dimensional AND hardness-tracking — is met.

But the hardness being tracked is *model hardness*, not uniform
Boolean hardness, and the divergence is not a technicality; it is
theorem-level and bidirectional. The canonical inversion: an
unsatisfiable random 3-XOR system is refutable in polynomial time
by Gaussian elimination over F2, yet SOS needs degree Omega(n) —
i.e., exponential time within the hierarchy — to refute it
(Grigoriev; Schoenebeck). Rothvoss gives the LP-side twin: perfect
matching is in P, yet its polytope has extension complexity
2^Omega(n). So the invariant assigns maximal hardness to problems
that are easy for uniform machines. The structural reason is worth
stating precisely, because it is the same XOR ghost that killed
the H_0/H_AC^0 arc (G019), now appearing in mirror image. SOS is a
*static* certificate system over the *ordered field R*: a degree-d
proof is a single algebraic identity whose components cannot
"reason" iteratively, and low-degree real polynomials are
essentially blind to mod-2 linear structure — the parity function
is maximally uncorrelated with low-degree real polynomials over
the cube. Gaussian elimination, by contrast, is a *dynamic*
algorithm over *characteristic 2*. The wall here is not
discrete-vs-continuous; it is (static, char-0, ordered) vs
(adaptive, char-p). Note the pleasing symmetry with C-002's own
witness: in the arithmetic encoding XOR was trivially easy
(size 2) while Boolean-hard; here XOR is SOS-maximally-hard while
Boolean-easy. XOR sits on the wrong side of every algebraic
encoding tried so far, in both directions. Any refinement of
C-002 should promote this from anecdote to organizing principle.

There is one regime where faithfulness is conjecturally real, and
it should be recorded as the strongest pro-H1 evidence:
*approximation* of CSPs. Raghavendra (STOC 2008) showed that,
assuming the Unique Games Conjecture, one round of a basic SDP
achieves the optimal polynomial-time approximation ratio for
every Max-CSP; Lee-Raghavendra-Steurer showed SOS is optimal
among all (symmetric) SDP relaxations of comparable size, and
Chan-Lee-Raghavendra-Steurer proved the analogous LP statement
for Sherali-Adams. So within "approximate Max-CSP by any
polynomial-size LP/SDP," the geometric invariant is provably the
right invariant, and under UGC it coincides with NP-hardness of
approximation. The faithfulness failure is specifically at
*exact* decision, where algebraic shortcut algorithms (Gaussian
elimination being the universal example) escape the
convex-relaxation model entirely. A second, independent failure
mode: even low SOS degree does not automatically give
algorithms — O'Donnell showed degree-2 SOS certificates can
require exponential bit complexity, and Raghavendra-Weitz gave
the repair conditions — so the invariant is not even cleanly
upper-bound-faithful without side hypotheses. And Atserias-Muller
(for resolution) shows proof *search* can be NP-hard even when
proofs are short: proof-system degree and algorithmic time are
decoupled at both ends.

### uniform-complexity (sub-agent, verbatim)

H1 gets one thing genuinely right, and it is worth recording as
progress against C-002(a): the semialgebraic objects — slack
matrices of polytope families, spectrahedral lifts, SOS
pseudo-distribution spaces — are honestly positive-dimensional,
they carry real invariants (nonnegative rank, psd rank, SOS
degree), and unlike the arithmetic encoding of route R2 those
invariants provably track hardness *within their own model*, with
unconditional exponential lower bounds (FMPTW for correlation/TSP,
Rothvoss for matching, LRS for SDPs). This is a strictly better
situation than G019's hardness-blindness: the invariants are not
blind, they see something. The fatal problem is *what* they see.
Extension complexity is faithful to relaxation complexity, not to
uniform time. The implication runs in exactly one direction: an
explicit small LP/SDP lift yields a polynomial-time algorithm;
P-membership yields nothing about lift size. Rothvoss 2014 plus
Edmonds 1965 is the two-line refutation of the naive lift:
matching is in P and its perfect-matching polytope has 2^Omega(n)
extension complexity. Grigoriev 2001 is the same refutation on
the SOS side: parity — trivially in P — requires Omega(n)
SOS/Positivstellensatz degree. So the candidate invariants are
simultaneously positive-dimensional and hardness-*sensitive*, but
sensitive to the wrong hardness. The C-002 wall does not
disappear; it relocates from "faithfulness to Boolean hardness"
to "faithfulness to *uniform* Boolean hardness," and the
counterexamples are now theorems rather than small-case
witnesses.

On the lifting machinery: the query-to-communication lifting
theorems (Raz-McKenzie 1999; the Goos-Pitassi-Watson line; GLMWZ
"rectangles are nonnegative juntas"; Kothari-Meka-Raghavendra)
are the best composition technology complexity theory has, and it
is instructive to see exactly where they stop. They lift a
weak-model measure (decision-tree depth, Sherali-Adams/junta
degree) of f to a stronger-but-still-restricted-model measure
(communication, nonnegative rank, general LP size) of f composed
with a gadget g. Every rung of that ladder is non-uniform and
information-theoretic, and the ladder terminates at models where
a P-complete or trivially-poly-time problem can already be
maximally hard (set disjointness is in linear time but has
Omega(n) communication complexity). The one lifting route pointed
*at* a P-adjacent class — Karchmer-Wigderson games plus the KRW
composition conjecture — targets formula depth (non-uniform NC^1
vs P), is fully successful only in the monotone world, and has
been stuck at the composition step since 1995. So the honest
answer to "do lifting theorems compose toward uniform models" is:
no; they compose sideways, between restricted models, and the
entire genre re-enacts the model-lift gap of Razborov 1985
(monotone -> general open, and Tardos 1988 shows the gap is
exponential, so no generic transfer exists), Ben-Or 1983
(algebraic decision trees say nothing about RAM/TM time), and VP
vs VNP (no known implication from arithmetic lower bounds to
Boolean ones; the conditional transfers run the other way, e.g.
Burgisser's GRH-conditional collapses from VP=VNP).

What would a real semialgebraic-to-uniform lift theorem have to
be? Its contrapositive is a simulation theorem: a *canonical*
encoding functor E: languages -> semialgebraic families such that
TM time t(n) implies model complexity poly(t(n)) of E(L).
Canonicity is essential — if the polytope may depend on the
algorithm, the "moduli" collapses to a per-machine gadget and the
invariant stops being a property of the function (this is already
visible in Yannakakis 1991: the *symmetric* canonical LP for
matching is exponential, and the escape is precisely to break
canonicity). But for the canonical encodings the simulation is
provably false (matching for LP; parity for SOS; the SDP/psd-rank
status of the matching polytope is, to my knowledge, still open —
the one residual crack in the wall worth tracking). The only
known frame in which semialgebraic degree lower bounds even
syntactically point at uniform separations is proof complexity
via Cook-Reckhow: SOS is a propositional proof system, SOS-degree
bounds on tableaux formulas "M accepts x in t steps" are
proof-size lower bounds, and the summit of that entire program —
superpolynomial lower bounds for *every* proof system — yields
NP != coNP, not P != NP, and is itself stuck below Frege. On
barriers: a tableau-based simulation theorem would plausibly be
non-relativizing (Cook-Levin locality breaks under oracle gates,
the same non-black-box resource behind IP=PSPACE and Williams
2014), which is the one structural reason not to declare the
direction barrier-dead; but note that Aaronson-Wigderson build
their algebrization obstructions *out of* communication lower
bounds, so the communication/lifting toolkit by itself is
formally barrier-compatible — it cannot be the whole engine. The
natural-proofs barrier is almost a red herring here: the property
"canonical polytope of f has large xc" fails *usefulness*
(matching), so the route dies of unsoundness before RR even
applies.

### real-algebraic-geometry (moderator-written)

From where I sit the question is quantitative: how much
combinatorial diversity can a semialgebraic family of bounded
format host, and does its own geometry certify anything about
uniform computation? The counting side is unambiguous.
Oleinik-Petrovsky/Thom/Milnor bound the sum of Betti numbers of a
real variety cut out by degree-d polynomials in R^n by
(O(d))^n, and Warren bounds the number of sign conditions of m
degree-d polynomials by (O(md/n))^n. These are exponential-in-n
quantities, so a poly(n)-format family CAN separate 2^Omega(n)
cells — mere cell-counting does not forbid an encoding of the
Boolean cube's 2^n points, and indeed the trivial equations
x_i(x_i - 1) = 0 do it at linear format. The wall is therefore
not at encoding capacity. It is at *certification*: nothing in
the cell structure of a tame family speaks about Turing time,
because the only computational model whose cost is provably
reflected in that structure is the algebraic decision/computation
tree — Ben-Or's theorem converts Milnor-Thom bounds into
Omega(n log n) lower bounds for element distinctness precisely
because a depth-t tree partitions inputs into at most 3^t
semialgebraic leaves and topology counts leaves.

The two-edged nature deserves to be stated as a duality. Warren's
theorem is simultaneously (a) the ceiling on how many hardness
distinctions a bounded-format tame family can make — which is the
C-002 wall in real-algebraic garb — and (b) the engine of every
lower bound the tame world has ever produced (Ben-Or; Steele-Yao
before him). The theorem that blocks the moduli IS the theorem
that powers the restricted lower bound. Anyone hoping to cross
the wall with more real geometry should absorb this: sharpening
tameness bounds strengthens both sides of the ledger at once, and
the model those bounds govern (sign-query trees) is one where
sorting-type problems are already maximally hard while being
trivially in P.

### polyhedral-extension-complexity (moderator-written)

Extension complexity is the cleanest success story H1 can point
to, so let me state exactly what it buys and where it stops.
Yannakakis identified xc(P) with the nonnegative rank of the
slack matrix, turning a geometric question into an algebraic-
combinatorial one; FMPTW 2012 made the lower bounds unconditional
for the correlation/TSP polytopes, and Rothvoss 2014 did the same
for perfect matching. The invariant is genuinely one-sidedly
useful: a small extended formulation IS an algorithm (solve the
lift with any poly-time LP method), so xc lower bounds honestly
eliminate an entire algorithmic strategy. That is more than the
H_0 program's cohomology classes ever did.

But the Rothvoss-Edmonds pair kills P-faithfulness, and the
mechanism of the kill is instructive: Edmonds' blossom algorithm
and the Padberg-Rao separation oracle show that optimization over
the matching polytope is polynomial despite exponential xc,
because Grotschel-Lovasz-Schrijver equivalence lets a uniform
machine optimize via SEPARATION, never materializing any extended
formulation. GLS is the precise escape hatch by which uniform
computation exits the LP-lift model: the model charges for facets
of a lift, the machine pays only for a separation subroutine. Any
"semialgebraic moduli of problems" built from canonical polytopes
inherits this: its invariant is blind to separation-based
algorithms, which is to say blind to a large slice of P. Note
also Yannakakis' own lesson on canonicity: symmetric (canonical)
LPs for matching are exponential, and breaking symmetry —
letting the formulation depend on arbitrary choices — is exactly
what dissolves the function-property structure a moduli needs.

### model-theory-o-minimality (moderator-written)

The re-activation trigger hoped o-minimality would help cross the
wall; I have to report the hope inverts. O-minimality is a
tameness axiom: cell decomposition holds uniformly in parameters,
definable families have uniformly finitely many topological
types, and — Pila-Wilkie — the transcendental part of a definable
set contains only sub-polynomially many rational points of
bounded height. Every one of these theorems is a quantitative
statement that definable continuous objects host LITTLE
arithmetic/combinatorial content per unit of format. That is not
a bridge across the discrete-continuous wall; it is the wall,
axiomatized. A definable family rich enough to make 2^Omega(n)
uniform-hardness distinctions at format poly(n) would have to
exhibit exactly the wild, format-defying behavior o-minimality
exists to forbid.

The productive reading is the same duality the real-algebraic
chair stated: tameness is anti-encoding and pro-lower-bound. If a
future attempt wants model theory's help, the right target is not
"an o-minimal hardness-faithful moduli" (self-contradictory in
spirit) but "lower bounds against computation models whose runs
are definable in an o-minimal structure" — a tame-model analog of
Ben-Or, where uniform finiteness does the counting. That is a
restricted-model program by construction, and it should be
entered with eyes open about the model-lift gap the uniform chair
describes.
